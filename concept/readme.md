<h1>CONCEPT</h1>
The Nautilus project is a hybrid architecture, which couples a solar panel with an internal electromagnetic pendulum generator. The selection of these sources is a temporal correlation in energy availability in the Venice lagoon context:<br><br>
<ul><li><b>Summer peak:</b> strong irradiation during summer period and calm water with small average height of the wave. In this period the solar panel provide the majority of energy.</li>
<li><b>Winter and bad weather peak: </b>kinetic energy from wave reach its peak during winter season and fog period, when there is more public and private transportation increasing the wave. In this period the pendulum provide the majority of energy.</li></ul>
The complementarity of this sources give us the possibility to significantly reduce the battery storage.
<h2>TECHNOLOGY SELECTION</h2>
During the initial design phase, we analyzed different source including piezoelectric, benthic microbial fuel cells and triboelectric nanogenerators. The combination solar panel with electromagnetic pendulum emerge as the only viable candidates for the Venice lagoon characteristics. For the low cost and maintenance in the buoy.
<h2>MECHANICAL ARCHITECTURE</h2>
We can divide the internal architecture in three parts:<br><br>
<ul><li>
 The upper section is a waterproof compartment containing the majority of the electronic part like the solar panel, the battery pack, the tp4056 and the sensor modules.
</li>
<li>
  The middle section contein the electromagnetic pendulum with a swing range of up to ± 30°. The pendulum has permanent magnets on the arm to create a moving magnetic field.
</li>
  <li>
    The bottom section serves a dual purpose: electromagnetic power generation and center of gravity management. The array of flat copper induction coils combined with the heavy magnet mass at the end of the pendulum keep the center of gravity below the center of buoyancy, this is important to avoid capsizing.
  </li>
</ul>
